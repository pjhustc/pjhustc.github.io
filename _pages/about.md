I am currently Staff Scientist at The Herbert Wertheim UF Scripps Institute. I am affliated in both Bioinformatics groups and UFIT Research Computing. Except for my role in supporting and collaborating with our research community, my research interest is to study how evolution give rise to novel protein structure, protein dynamics, and protein-protein interactions (PPI). This is critical to understand how evolutionary new add-ons shape the complex cellular machinary and drive phenotypical novelty. Additionally, it provides insights into the relations between evolutionary new genes and health/diseases. To achieve this, I use a set of special genes, known as <i>de novo</i> genes, as these genes enable us to better track their origination and evolution compared to other genes. My work involves three directions: (1) <b>Developing comprehensive computational pipelines</b> that integrate comparative genomics, multi-omics, and population genomics to identify <i>de novo</i> genes in <i>Drosophila melangoaster</i> (fruit fly), humans, and other species; (2) <b>Applying large scale computational biophysics</b> to study the <i>de novo</i> protein structures and their protein-protein interactions; and (3) <b>Combining molecular evolution and machine learning techniques</b> to study how evolution generates novel protein structures and PPIs, and how these innovations shape the complex cellular machinary.
